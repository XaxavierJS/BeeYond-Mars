## High-level summary
BeeYond Mars is an educational pixel-art adventure about friendship, engineering and a second chance at science. Help Lum reunite a research team and design a spacecraft for a hypothetical dormant-bumblebee study on the way to Mars. Balance mass, power, cost and communications; compare route scenarios; handle flight incidents; and use the mission report to understand the outcome and redesign. The game turns mission trade-offs into decisions players can see and discuss.

## Detailed project description
Space mission design brings many competing requirements together. For learners, those relationships can remain abstract when each subsystem is taught separately. BeeYond Mars gives players a small, complete mission to reason about, wrapped in an approachable research story.

Lum's laboratory is close to shutting down when a letter arrives from Aurelia, a fictional research agency. Players explore, reconnect with Pip, Nim and Eko, and prepare a hypothetical study involving dormant bumblebee queens. They choose a biological chamber and equipment, compare launcher/cruise-bus packages and Mars route scenarios, and obtain additional fictional funding through design reviews. Resource checks explain whether the design meets mass, generation, budget, communications and delta-v constraints.

After launch, a deterministic voyage introduces incidents that require decisions. Installed equipment, repair charges, battery reserves and communications capability shape the available responses. The report explains the resulting technical outcome, while redesign allows another attempt. A sealed greenhouse and later imagined award extend the story as fiction. One to four players can share a device and responsibilities locally.

## How we address Space Mission Design Game
The game connects mission objective, payload, spacecraft equipment, launcher choice, route selection, operations and evaluation. Players manage limited resources and see how changing one part affects the rest of the design. Failure is followed by explanation and another design opportunity. Exploration, team conversations and a research notebook connect the technical work to the people making it.

## Educational value and intended audience
The project is intended for learners interested in space and science who benefit from experimenting with a complete mission. It invites comparison, resource planning, interpretation of constraints and evidence-based redesign. We have not measured learning gains with an external study and do not claim validated educational effectiveness.

## Technology and implementation
Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no runtime backend or external npm dependencies. Mission rules, world exploration, rendering, assessment, data and audio are separate modules. Browser local storage preserves progress. The game includes keyboard and on-screen controls, language selection, adjustable decision timing, independent audio controls and reduced-motion support.

## Testing and limitations
Public Pages checks covered 120 resources and desktop/mobile layouts. A browser run from prepared checkpoints covered launch, flight decisions, arrival, report, four greenhouse decisions, ending and redesign without observed JavaScript exceptions. Four current story tests pass. A legacy engine test suite still references deleted files and must be adapted; the full suite does not pass. The complete opening narrative without checkpoints and physical mobile/assistive-technology testing remain pending.

This is an educational model, not a flight simulator. Risk is a game index, not a survival probability. Terrestrial diapause studies and historical honeybee experiments in orbit do not demonstrate interplanetary survival of bumblebee queens. Launcher capacities, credits, power rules, linear mass penalty, incidents and outcomes are simplified team-defined values. Human travel, the greenhouse and the award ending are fictional.
