# BeeYond Mars — submission draft

## Project summary
A discouraged researcher receives a second chance from the fictional Aurelia agency. Reunite with three friends, design a spacecraft for a hypothetical dormant bumblebee payload, and learn how mass, power, budget, communication and time constrain each other. A retro adventure turns engineering decisions into a shared research story. Failure produces an explainable report and a chance to redesign, not an arbitrary game over.

## Addressing Space Mission Design Game
Players define a biological objective, choose a chamber and instruments, compare launcher/cruise-bus packages and route scenarios, earn design-review funding, check constraints, respond to seeded incidents and interpret results. One to four players collaborate locally on a shared device. The research journal links each milestone to a story memory. The greenhouse and imagined Nobel ending are explicitly fictional continuations.

## NASA data and scientific honesty
The NASA Mars Fact Sheet gives a Mars/Earth irradiance ratio of 0.431. Version 13 uses an explicitly simplified 0.85 cruise-generation factor for game balance; it is not an orbital or surface power model. The NASA Ames Trajectory Browser informs the route design; the exact route numbers are transcribed from the team's source project and their query provenance still needs verification. ESA signal-delay information motivates onboard autonomy. Terrestrial bumblebee research is context, not proof of survival in space. Risk is a game index, not a survival percentage.

## Implementation and verification
Browser-native HTML/CSS/JavaScript; no installation, backend, payment or account required by the game. Pure deterministic rules, semantic controls, adjustable timing, autosave, motion reduction and original procedural chiptune. Automated tests cover resource accounting, failure, retry, timers, role permissions and feasible routes. Desktop checks exercised the v13 prologue, navigation, Pip repair, Nim care and saved memories; the simulation and ending classification are covered by automated tests. A full v13 interface playthrough and mobile testing remain pending.

## A 30-second demo plan — record the actual game
- 0–4 seconds: Aurelia's letter and the 2,000-credit grant. “A small life. A second chance at science.”
- 4–10 seconds: Select Vanguard and compare launcher power. “Design a mission where every resource matters.”
- 10–16 seconds: Show the arrival-power check and a valid launch, labelled as a simplified model.
- 16–22 seconds: Resolve a mechanical incident and show its cost.
- 22–27 seconds: Show the mission report and research journal.
- 27–30 seconds: Team and bee in the imagined greenhouse. “Carry a question farther. BeeYond Mars.”

Keep the final file at or below 30 seconds, including title and ending. Use English voice/text or English subtitles. This is a storyboard, not an already-recorded demo.
